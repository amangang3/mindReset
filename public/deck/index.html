What we did

Did a 2-minute calming track actually calm someone down?

A within-subject A/B against the participant's own natural recovery.

Phase 1 — Baseline arm Recover with nothing.
  1. Rest baseline measurement
  2. Hyperventilate for 30 seconds
  3. Measure at peak
  4. Sit quietly for 30 seconds — no intervention
  5. Measure recovery (control)
→
Phase 2 — Intervention arm Recover with the track.
  1. Hyperventilate for 30 seconds
  2. Measure at peak
  3. Play calming track for 30 seconds
  4. Measure post-intervention
Measures captured
HR bpm SUDS 0–10 Garmin Stress ↑ = more stress EEG AWEAR Vibes AI score

Manual entry at each of five timepoints: rest, P1 peak, P1 natural, P2 peak, P2 post.

Why this design
  • Same person, same stressor. The participant is their own control.
  • Phase 1 natural recovery is the floor: how much do they come back without help?
  • Phase 2 intervention recovery measures the lift above that floor.
  • The gap between the two = the actual effect of the intervention.

Results & interpretation

Vibes AI score: +21 lift from the intervention.

30 seconds of calming audio in the 40 Hz / 528 Hz family produced a measurable score jump — beyond what natural recovery delivered.

Reading the table
  • HR recovered fully under both conditions (~70 bpm). The body returned to baseline either way.
  • Garmin Stress (higher = more stress) fell further with the intervention: 55 → 24 vs 55 → 28 in natural recovery. Both ended below the resting baseline of 31; intervention went deeper.
  • Vibes AI is where the headline lift surfaces: 28 → 49 after the intervention vs flat 28 → 28 in natural recovery.
Honest caveats. n = 1, single session, fixed order (Phase 1 always first), operator entered values manually. The "HRV" row in the screenshot is Garmin's composite stress score, not raw HRV ms — higher means more stressed. Track family was layered brown noise — broader 40 Hz / 528 Hz frequencies are the research direction, not the exact stimulus tested here. SUDS and EEG were not captured this run.
Anchor · What's next 3 / 3
Implications & next steps

A pocket-deployable, measurable calming intervention.

If 30 seconds of sound can shift the Vibes AI score by 20+ points and drop Garmin stress further than natural recovery, the same loop can ride on any wearable signal.

Implications
  • Short windows are enough. The lift came in 30 seconds — no full meditation required for a measurable effect.
  • Within-subject A/B is the right unit. Comparing intervention against the same person's natural recovery filters out the "they calmed down anyway" critique.
  • Vibes AI is sensitive. The score moved when HR and HRV had already returned — useful as an early-detection metric.
  • The panic-button thesis holds. A 30-second calming loop is feasible as a lock-screen interrupt for anxiety spikes.
Production roadmap
  1. Live EEG via AWEAR — replace manual transcription with streaming behind-ear electrodes.
  2. HRV from a chest strap — rolling RMSSD instead of single readings.
  3. Auto-trigger on baseline return — end the intervention when biometrics cross back, not on a fixed timer.
  4. Panic-button launch — lock-screen gesture opens the intervention and tracks recovery against a saved personal baseline.
  5. Vibes AI integration — the score is the headline metric; build the score-delta into the product loop.
Next study (to make this evidence, not just signal)
  • nn > 1, counterbalanced order. Half the participants run intervention arm first to wash out order effects.
  • ASham-track control. Match duration with neutral audio so the effect is isolated to the calming frequencies.
  • HzStimulus comparison. Brown noise vs 40 Hz binaural vs 528 Hz solfeggio — find which frequency family carries the Vibes AI lift.
  • ↻Repeated sessions. Does the effect strengthen with practice, or habituate?
